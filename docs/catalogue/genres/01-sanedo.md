## 1. Genre Distinction & Musical Identity

### 1.1 What is Sanedo?
Sanedo is one of the most vibrant and distinctive folk musical forms of Gujarat. Originating in the Patan and Mehsana districts of North Gujarat, Sanedo was historically performed during festival nights, village squares, and wedding celebrations. It was introduced to modern mass recording and revolutionized by the late legendary folk singer **Maniraj Barot**, and later brought to global concert stages and rock/fusion format by **Arvind Vegda** (*"Bhala Mori Rama / Bhai Bhai"*).

### 1.2 Structure & Rhythm
- **The Couplet (Doha):** Every verse begins with an improvised or traditional rhyming couplet (doha) of 2 lines. The lyricism covers social humor, romantic banter, village everyday life, or fervent devotion to Maa Ambaji, Khodiyar Maa, or Chamunda Maa.
- **The Call-and-Response Refrain:** Following the two lines of the doha, the entire circle erupts into the high-energy chant:
  > *"Sanedo... Sanedo... Lal Lal Sanedo!*  
  > *Amba Maa Na Dham Ma Sanedo..."*
- **Tempo & Instrumentation:** Typically played in a brisk, punchy 4-beat or 2-beat folk meter driving rapid hand claps, dhol, dholak, shehnai, and harmonium, accented today with brass, synthesizers, or rock guitar rhythms.
- **Dance Style:** Unlike traditional circular clapping garba (Tran Taali / Be Taali) or stick-striking Dandiya, Sanedo is danced with free-form vertical jumping, synchronized dipping, rapid foot stomps, and circle expansion/contraction. It universally serves as the high-octane climax of Navratri nights (usually performed after midnight as the final dance segment).

### 1.3 Invariants: What belongs here vs. elsewhere
- **MUST have:** The distinct Sanedo couplet meter + *"Sanedo Sanedo"* refrain, or be an explicit Sanedo folk/anthem composition.
- **DO NOT mix with:**
  - Standard Tran Taali (3-clap) or Be Taali (2-clap) Garba (which belong in `traditional-garba`).
  - Stick-based Dandiya Raas (which belongs in `raas-dandiya`).
  - Sacred trance percussion without the couplet refrain (which belongs in `dakla`).
