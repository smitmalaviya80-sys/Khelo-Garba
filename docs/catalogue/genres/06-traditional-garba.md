## 1. Genre Distinction & Musical Identity

### 1.1 What is Traditional Garba?
Garba (ગરબા) derives etymologically from the Sanskrit word *Garbha* (womb) and *Garbha Deep* (a sacred lantern enclosed within a perforated earthen pot). The dance represents the eternal cycle of life, death, and rebirth moving in concentric circular orbits around the central flame of the Divine Mother (Maa Jagdamba). 

### 1.2 Sub-Distinctions

#### A. Tran Taali (3-Taali / 3-Clap)
- **Meter:** Set in *Dadra Taal* (6 beats) or *Keherwa Taal* (8 beats) with a distinct 3-clap pattern: Clap 1 (center-right), Clap 2 (center-left), Clap 3 (overhead or turn), followed by a 1-beat rest/pivot.
- **Movement:** Dancers form large concentric rings, stepping forward into the circle on the first two claps and pivoting outward on the third.
- **Pioneers:** **Atul Purohit** (the sovereign master of Vadodara Tran Taali), **Hemant Chauhan**, **Praful Dave**, **Achal Mehta**.

#### B. Be Taali (2-Taali / 2-Clap)
- **Meter:** Steady, flowing 4-beat or 8-beat cycle (Clap-Clap-Step).
- **Movement:** Graceful, sweeping, continuous circular progression without sharp turns; favored in traditional courtyard, temple, and village gatherings.
- **Pioneers:** Traditional Prachin Mandlis, Hemant Chauhan, Diwaliben Bhil.

#### C. Prachin Garbi (Men's Traditional Heritage Garbi)
- **Context:** Historically sung and danced exclusively by men around wooden mandaps or brass lamps in Saurashtra. Vigorous leaps, sword/shield stances, and intricate high-vocal hymns composed by Dayaram, Vallabh Mevada, and Narsinh Mehta.

### 1.3 Invariants: What belongs here vs. elsewhere
- **MUST have:** Circular clapping hand choreography without sticks; devotional or cultural lyrical grounding.
- **DO NOT mix with:**
  - Stick dance (`raas-dandiya`).
  - Sanedo couplets (`sanedo`).
  - Trance percussion without circular clapping (`dakla`).
