NAVRATRI + GARBA

Nine nights. One circle around Shakti.

In Gujarat, Garba is one of the defining ways communities gather during Navratri.

01 · NINE NIGHTS

Navratri literally means “nine nights”.

Navratri is celebrated in many parts of India, but Gujarat is especially associated with nine nights of public and community dance. Gujarat Tourism describes villages and cities gathering in open spaces throughout the state during the festival.

Garba is not an entertainment layer added to Navratri. Its devotional meaning is part of why the dance belongs to the festival.

02 · SHAKTI

The festival centres feminine divinity.

UNESCO describes Garba as being performed during Navratri, a festival dedicated to the worship of feminine energy, or Shakti. The mother goddess Amba is one of the central devotional references within the Garba tradition.

03 · THE GARBO

The centre of the circle is meaningful.

A traditional Garba circle may form around a perforated earthenware pot illuminated by an oil lamp, or around an image of the mother goddess. Gujarat Tourism also describes a community shrine and garbo as a focal point of the festival.

This is why the visual language of a circle around a lit centre appears so often in descriptions of Garba. It reflects the structure of the practice itself.

04 · COMMUNITY

The festival is carried by more than dancers.

UNESCO identifies dancers, musicians, social groups, craftspeople and religious figures among the practitioners and bearers of Garba. Public events may be large, but the tradition also continues through neighbourhoods, families, villages and smaller community gatherings.

The shared night is part of the tradition: people gather, sing, clap, dance, prepare spaces, make garments and ornaments, and pass the practice on by participation.
