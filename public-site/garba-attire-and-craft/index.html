ATTIRE + CRAFT

Garba is made as well as danced.

Clothing, ornaments, pottery and other craft traditions help give Garba its material identity.

01 · THE MAKERS

Garba's cultural system includes craftspeople.

UNESCO identifies craftspeople among the practitioners and bearers who sustain Garba. Its safeguarding material specifically refers to costume-making and pottery alongside music and dance.

That means the visual identity of Garba is not separate from the tradition. It is made by people whose work supports the gatherings themselves.

02 · CLOTHING

There is no single permanent Garba uniform.

Garba clothing varies by region, community, event and period. Embroidery, mirror work, textile colour and movement-friendly silhouettes are common visual associations, but styles continue to change.

For a cultural guide, it is safer to recognise that range than to claim one outfit is the only authentic way to dress for Garba.

03 · ORNAMENTS + OBJECTS

Dress sits beside pottery and festival objects.

UNESCO's description links the Garba ecosystem with garments, ornaments and the making of the garbha-deep. Together, these objects make the festival visible as well as audible.

That is why PlayGarba's approved image library includes craftspeople, textiles, instruments and ritual objects rather than only dancers on a stage.

04 · CHANGING STYLES

New dress can still belong to a living tradition.

UNESCO noted during inscription that every generation redefines Garba through new performing styles, lyrics and dress while retaining its cultural centre. Clothing changes are therefore part of the documented evolution of the form.
