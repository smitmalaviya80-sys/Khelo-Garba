DANDIYA RAAS

The sticks are part of the dance, not decoration.

Dandiya Raas is a Gujarati group dance built around paired wooden sticks, circular movement and rhythmic exchange.

01 · THE STICKS

Dandiya creates rhythm through contact.

Gujarat Tourism describes Dandiya Raas as a circular group dance performed with small wooden sticks. Dancers strike their own or a partner's sticks as they move, turning the sticks into part of both the choreography and the audible rhythm.

This is the quickest practical clue for identifying Dandiya Raas. If the dance is organised around paired sticks, you are looking at a Raas form rather than ordinary Garba clapping.

02 · RAAS

It belongs to a broader Gujarati Raas tradition.

Official Gujarat tourism material connects Raas traditions particularly with Saurashtra and Kutch and with Krishna and Gop cultural references. Modern Navratri programmes may present Dandiya Raas alongside Garba, but its movement vocabulary and cultural references remain distinct.

03 · RHYTHM

The pattern depends on timing with other dancers.

The sticks make coordination visible and audible. Dancers move through repeating patterns, meeting partners or lines at the correct beat. Different events and communities use different steps, so there is no single universal Dandiya sequence.

04 · GARBA + DANDIYA

They share Navratri nights, not one identity.

Large Navratri gatherings often move between Garba and Dandiya Raas. That is why the words are sometimes used loosely for the same evening. PlayGarba keeps Dandiya as a distinct listening category in Explore, so the distinction does not disappear inside the catalogue.
